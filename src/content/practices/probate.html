What probate involves

Probate is the court-supervised process used to identify and manage a deceased person’s property, address valid debts, and distribute the remaining estate. Whether probate is required depends on how assets were owned, their value, beneficiary designations, and whether a trust or other transfer method applies. The firm helps families determine the correct process and understand the steps ahead.

Executor and administrator duties

The court-appointed personal representative has legal duties to the estate and its beneficiaries. Those responsibilities may include securing property, obtaining valuations, maintaining insurance, keeping records, managing accounts, and seeking court approval for certain actions. The attorneys guide representatives through filings, hearings, inventories, reports, and decisions that arise during administration.

Notice, creditors, and estate assets

California probate includes formal notice requirements and a process for creditor claims. Assets must be identified, collected, valued, and sometimes sold. Real property, businesses, contested ownership, or incomplete records can add complexity. The firm helps address those issues and coordinates with accountants, appraisers, brokers, and other professionals when appropriate.

Distribution and closing the estate

Before final distribution, the representative must resolve expenses and claims, account for estate activity, and obtain required approvals. The firm prepares the necessary petitions and supports a clear distribution process. It also represents beneficiaries or fiduciaries when disagreements develop over a will, property, accounting, or the conduct of administration.

Support after a death

Legal deadlines can be difficult to manage while a family is grieving. A prompt review can identify urgent property concerns, determine whether a smaller-estate procedure is available, and prevent avoidable delays in the larger process.
