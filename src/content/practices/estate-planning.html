Powers of attorney and health care directives

An estate plan should address decisions during life as well as the transfer of property after death. A durable power of attorney can authorize a trusted person to manage financial matters if needed, while an advance health care directive records medical preferences and identifies someone to make health decisions. The firm explains the authority each document provides and helps clients select arrangements that fit their family and circumstances.

Wills and trusts

Wills and revocable living trusts can direct how property is managed and distributed, identify responsible decision-makers, and support planning for minor children or beneficiaries who need continued oversight. The right structure depends on assets, family relationships, tax considerations, privacy goals, and the complexity of administration.

The firm prepares coordinated plans rather than isolated forms. Beneficiary designations, title to property, business interests, and retirement accounts should be reviewed alongside the core documents.

Asset and succession planning

Planning may include real property, closely held businesses, blended families, charitable goals, and strategies for transferring management or ownership. The attorneys help clients identify potential conflicts, clarify responsibilities, and document a plan that can be understood and carried out.

Plans should also be revisited after major life changes such as marriage, divorce, a birth or death, a significant purchase or sale, or a move to another state.

Trust administration

After a death or incapacity, a trustee must follow the governing document and California law. The firm advises trustees on notices, records, debts, taxes, asset management, distributions, and communications with beneficiaries. It also helps beneficiaries understand their rights and addresses disputes when questions arise about administration.
