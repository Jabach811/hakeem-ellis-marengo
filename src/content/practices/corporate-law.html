Formation and governance

A sound corporate structure should reflect how the business will actually operate. The firm helps clients evaluate entity choices, organize corporations and limited liability companies, define ownership interests, and document decision-making authority. That work may include articles and operating documents, bylaws, shareholder or member agreements, resolutions, and records needed to keep the company in good standing.

Clear governance is especially important when ownership is shared. Well-drafted agreements can establish voting rights, transfer restrictions, buyout terms, management duties, and a process for resolving deadlocks before they threaten the business.

Contracts and transactions

The firm prepares, reviews, and negotiates agreements used in day-to-day operations and major transactions. Matters may involve vendor and customer contracts, confidentiality provisions, leases, purchase and sale agreements, financing documents, and the acquisition or disposition of business assets.

Counsel focuses on the terms that matter in practice: what each party must do, when performance is due, how risk is allocated, and what happens if the relationship changes or the agreement is breached.

Employment and operating documents

Corporate clients may need agreements and policies that support consistent operations. The firm assists with employment-related documents, independent-contractor agreements, confidentiality and proprietary-information provisions, and internal approvals. When specialized tax, benefits, or regulatory advice is needed, counsel can help the client identify and coordinate with the right professional.

Dissolution and corporate disputes

Changes in ownership, financial pressure, or a breakdown among principals can require restructuring or dissolution. The firm helps clients evaluate contractual duties, corporate records, assets, liabilities, and available resolution paths. When negotiation is not enough, its litigation experience supports a coordinated approach to corporate and ownership disputes.
